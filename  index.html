1. Origin of Chess and Grandpa's Philosophy — We Do Not Move Pieces We Move Ideas

Chess originated in India as Chaturanga around 6th century, spread to Persia as Shatranj, then to Europe. Grandpa Donald Weiss learned chess in New York parks, sitting next to older players in sweaters — whole point is sitting next to you, not computer. Grandpa's core philosophy: We do not move pieces, we move ideas. Every move should have an idea.

2. Control Center — Center Pawns First

Control center with pawns and pieces together. Center pawns e4 and d4 are most important first moves. Pawn on e4 attacks d5 and f5, pawn on d4 attacks c5 and e5 — together they control 4 center squares and give pieces room. Knights in center attack 8 squares, knights on edge attack 4 — so put knights in center.

3. Development — Knights Before Bishops — Don't Move Same Piece Twice

Develop knights first to f3, c3, f6, c6 — knights find best squares first. Then bishops — bishops need to know where knights are before choosing best diagonal. Don't move same piece twice in opening — waste of tempo — develop new piece each move.

4. King Safety — Castle Early — Don't Leave King in Center

King in center is target — castle early within first 10 moves — usually kingside — king goes to g1 or g8 safe, rook comes to center file e or d. After castling, rooks see each other — rooks connected.

5. Don't Hang Pieces — Count Attackers vs Defenders

Hanging piece means piece not protected — free for opponent. Before you move, look: Is my piece protected? Count attackers vs defenders on square you want to move.

6. Take Free Pieces — Material Wins

If opponent hangs piece, take it — don't be polite — material wins games. Queen 9 points, rook 5 points, bishop 3 points, knight 3 points, pawn 1 point.

7. Check, Capture, Threat — Forcing Moves First

Every position, look at forcing moves first in order: Checks — does check win material or mate? Then Captures — does capture win material? Then Threats — what threat can I make that opponent must answer?

8. Pawn Structure — Avoid Doubled Isolated Backward

Avoid doubled pawns — two pawns same file can't defend each other. Avoid isolated pawns — pawn with no friendly pawns on adjacent files — weak. Avoid backward pawns — pawn behind friendly pawns can't advance — weak. Keep pawns connected.

9. Rooks — Connect Rooks — Open Files — Rook Behind Passed Pawn

Rooks belong on open files — files with no pawns — rooks see whole board on open files. Connect rooks — move queen off back rank so rooks see each other. Rook behind passed pawn supports its advance.

10. Endgame — King Active — Opposition — Passed Pawns Push

In endgame king is fighting piece — centralize king — king to center becomes strong, helps pawns queen. Opposition — kings two squares apart facing — who moves loses opposition. Passed pawns push — passed pawn dangerous — push quickly. Lucena position — bridge building — Philidor defense — draw.

Origin of Chess — History — Chaturanga to Modern

Chess originated in India around 6th century AD as Chaturanga — four divisions of army: infantry pawn, cavalry knight, elephantry bishop, chariotry rook. Spread to Persia as Shatranj — Persian word Shah means king, Shah Mat means king helpless — checkmate. Arabs spread to Europe via Spain and Sicily — 10th century. Modern rules developed around 1475 in Spain — queen became powerful, bishop moves diagonal unlimited. First world champion Wilhelm Steinitz 1886. Grandpa Donald Weiss learned in Washington Square Park New York 1950s — sitting next to older Jewish men in sweaters smoking — whole point is sitting next to you in sweater warm tea board not computer.

Rules of Chess — Complete

Chess board 8x8 64 squares light and dark — white on right — white moves first. Pieces: King moves 1 square any direction — cannot move into check — if king in check must get out. Queen moves any number squares horizontal vertical diagonal — most powerful 9 points. Rook moves horizontal vertical any number — 5 points — castle with king. Bishop moves diagonal any number — 3 points — bishop stays on color. Knight moves L shape 2+1 jumps over pieces — 3 points — only piece that jumps — fork powerful. Pawn moves forward 1 square, first move 2 squares, captures diagonal 1 square — en passant capture if pawn moves 2 squares next to opponent pawn — promotion when pawn reaches 8th rank becomes queen rook bishop knight usually queen. Castling: king moves 2 squares toward rook, rook jumps over — cannot castle through check or into check or if pieces between or if king or rook moved or if in check — kingside castling short, queenside long. Checkmate: king in check cannot move block capture — game ends. Stalemate: player not in check but no legal moves — draw.

Opening Principles

Opening principles: Control center, develop knights before bishops, castle early, connect rooks, don't move same piece twice, don't bring queen out early, don't hang pieces. Don't memorize long lines — understand ideas — Grandpa's favorite center 1.e4 e5 2.d4 exd4 3.Qxd4 — simple center opens center quickly. Ideas not pieces — every opening move should have idea — e4 idea control center and open bishop queen, Nf3 idea develop knight attack e5 control center. Memorize ideas not moves — ideas last, moves forget.

Middlegame Plans

Middlegame after opening — find plan — not just move pieces — have plan. Plans: Open file for rook — rook belongs on open file file with no pawns — rook on open file sees whole board. Outpost for knight — outpost is square where knight can't be chased by opponent pawn — knight on outpost strong. Bishop pair — two bishops vs bishop+knight or two knights — bishop pair strong in open position where diagonals open — keep bishop pair. Space advantage — if you have more space pawns advanced control more squares — don't give up space — push. Pawn break — pawn move that opens position — e.g., d4 break in closed center — pawn break changes structure — know when to break.

Endgame Knowledge

Endgame after many pieces exchanged — king becomes fighting piece — centralize king — king active — king to center — opposition — kings two squares apart facing each other — who moves loses opposition — key to king and pawn endings — if you have opposition you can force opponent to give way. Passed pawn — pawn with no opponent pawns in front on same or adjacent files — passed pawn dangerous must push — passed pawns must be pushed quickly — opponent must stop. Rook behind passed pawn — rook supports passed pawn from behind — rook in front blocks own pawn — so rook behind best. Lucena position — winning rook and pawn vs rook — bridge building — build bridge with rook to shelter king from checks — Lucena wins. Philidor position — defending rook and pawn vs rook — Philidor defense draws — rook stays on third rank cuts king off.

Tactics Awareness

Tactics win material — must know patterns. Fork one piece attacks two — knight fork most common. Pin piece can't move because king or valuable behind — absolute pin king behind can't move illegal, relative pin queen behind can move but loses queen. Skewer opposite pin valuable front less valuable behind — force valuable to move win piece behind. Discovered attack move one piece away reveal attack from piece behind — discovered check very powerful. Hanging piece not protected any piece — take it. Back rank mate king trapped on back rank by own pawns rook or queen delivers mate on back rank. Smothered mate knight delivers mate king surrounded own pieces can't escape. Deflection force opponent piece away from defending important square or piece. See tactics pane with board diagrams illustrating each tactic — board diagrams — clear explanation.
